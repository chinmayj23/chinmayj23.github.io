Chinmay Joshi

I am interested in the science of deep learning, especially how models learn, behave, and make use of data. My work focuses on understanding these systems more clearly, with the goal of making AI more efficient, reliable, and trustworthy.

I recently completed a master's degree in Computer Science at Saarland University, where I focused on deep learning, computer vision, and data science. For my master's thesis at the CISPA Helmholtz Center for Information Security, I worked with Dr. Rebekka Burkholz on data pruning, model memorization, and privacy. The project studied how pruning changes what a model memorizes and what this means for privacy in learned systems. Before that, I was a research intern at the National Institute of Informatics in Tokyo, where I worked with Dr. Satoshi Ikehata on relighting and 3D reconstruction from a single image.

Alongside my education, I also worked with the German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence and Fraunhofer IZFP on topics such as interpretable subgroup discovery, urban mobility, and geospatial data analysis. Before moving to Germany, I worked on machine vision pipelines at Aurify Systems and began my research work at NMIMS with Dr. Siba Panda, where I explored questions at the intersection of data science and finance.

When I'm not distracted by these endless academic pursuits, I'm busy pondering philosophy or trying to predict the next Wimbledon champion. Sometimes, you might find me in the mountains with my camera or deep in the forest, lost in the world of birds.
